Anna Chernesky

Pre-doc at Princeton University & UBC graduate

I'm a pre-doctoral research assistant in Princeton University's Industrial Relations Section, working for Pauline Carry and Owen Zidar. My interests lie at the intersection of labor, behavioral, and cultural economics. I am most interested in studying how constraints shape individuals' labor market entry, how this can alter belief systems and prosocial behavior, and what the implications are for inequality.

Before my pre-doc, I was working at the Bank of Canada researching cash usage in Canada. I received my BA in Honours Economics from the University of British Columbia in Vancouver, and have completed coursework at the University of Ottawa and Princeton University.

Research interests

  • Occupational choice I am interested in studying factors influencing the occupations individuals end up in, and the role individual beliefs and binding constraints play. Subtopics of interest include monopsonistic labor markets and limited opportunity, access to college, parental occupational inheritance, and more.
  • Intercultural contact I am interested in studying how circumstance around intercultural contact can affect prejudice or bias between individuals or groups.
  • Survey Design More of a methodological interest than a research topic, I am interested in leveraging survey data to answer the types of questions I am interested in.
