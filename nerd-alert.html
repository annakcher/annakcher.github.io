Nerd Alert

Best movies

All-time

  • Titanic James Cameron, 1997. Does not deserve the hate it's accrued over the years. This is an excellent multi-genre film and deserves all its accolades.
  • Chicago Rob Marshall, 2002. Just pure entertainment and an excellent commentary on the media circus.
  • The Umbrellas of Cherbourg Jacques Demy, 1964. The world hasn't seen color like this since.
  • Zodiac David Fincher, 2007. Fantastically directed. I have never been so spooked.
  • No Other Choice Park Chan-wook, 2025. A labor economist must-watch (I'm not endorsing the methods used in this film to obtain a job).
  • Portrait of a Lady on Fire Céline Sciamma, 2019. Slow, haunting, heartbreaking, and beautiful.
  • Whiplash Damien Chazelle, 2014. Obsession can only go so far before it becomes a problem (I really don't think Fletcher's methods are justified in the end).

Recent watches

  • The Prestige (Nolan, 2006) Watched for the first time this month and was entranced. I love a movie about magic. Layer Nolan's filmmaking on top of that and there's bound to be something good.
  • Red Rooms (Plante, 2023) Québécois thriller, extremely effective at creating a dark atmosphere. Highly recommend for fans of Zodiac.

Best books

All-time

  • Pillars of the Earth (Follett) Sometimes the best tale is a simple one. Excellent historical epic.
  • Anna Karenina (Tolstoy) One of the best classics for a reason.
  • Pachinko (Lee) Multigenerational epic and historical. Had me crying at times.

Recent reads

  • East of Eden (Steinbeck) Love a multigenerational epic. I haven't gotten around to reading any Steinbeck before and this was a good introduction.
  • Atmosphere (Reid) This had me crying more than a little bit.
  • All the Lovers in the Night (Kawakami) Descriptions of isolation and loneliness that felt so personal!
